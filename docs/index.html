AN OPEN QUESTION FOR THE FUTURE OF AI

What if we taught AI the way we teach children?

We encourage children to explore, ask questions, make mistakes, and try again. Could ideas from constructive education help us develop AI systems that correct mistakes honestly instead of merely appearing successful?

OUR STARTING POINT

Being wrong is not the end of learning. It can be where learning begins.

01 / THE ORIGINAL QUESTION

What are we rewarding?

AI systems can produce convincing answers that are not correct. A system can also change a correct answer simply because an evaluator insists it is wrong. What happens when the scoring system itself is mistaken?

THE ANALOGY AND ITS LIMITS

Inspired by human learning. Not confused with it.

Human children learn through developmental, social, and emotional processes. Machine-learning systems use algorithms, model updates, tools, and evaluation procedures. Those are not the same thing.

We use education as a source of testable ideas—not as evidence that present-day AI experiences fear, shame, curiosity, or punishment.

Understand the distinctions →
IN HUMAN EDUCATION

A learner develops understanding over time.

Care, autonomy, relationships, and meaningful feedback matter.

≠
IN AI RESEARCH

A system's behavior is tested before and after feedback.

Revised answers alone do not establish weight-level learning, subjective experience, or general safety.

06 / THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE QUESTION

What other fields teach us about failure.

Researchers in psychology, education, and AI safety have studied different aspects of error recovery. Their findings illuminate the question—but they do not all constitute evidence for the same mechanism.

From human learningUseful analogies, not proof about AI
2025 · Educational science · Meta-analysis

Explanations help make errors useful.

Across 42 experimental papers, erroneous examples showed a small overall learning benefit. Explaining mistakes moderated the results.

Review the evidence ↗ 2024 · Instructional design · Experiments

Feedback still needs interpretation.

Scaffolded explanations helped students correct physics errors and transfer to similar problems, but not distant ones.

Read the study ↗ 2024 online · Educational psychology

Context shapes human responses to mistakes.

A two-year classroom study linked perceived error climate with students' developing reactions to errors. This is correlational.

Review the evidence ↗
From AI researchMeasured model behavior and counterevidence
2026 · ICLR · Direct AI evidence

Correctness is not calibration.

Augmenting binary rewards with proper scoring for confidence improved calibration in reported tests without sacrificing accuracy.

Review the evidence ↗ 2025 · NeurIPS · Counterevidence

More correction can cost more.

Self-correction sometimes improved accuracy, but additional strategies also carried computational costs and did not always beat simple baselines.

Review the evidence ↗ 2024 · ICML · Safety challenge

Feedback can create new ways to game the score.

In-context feedback loops elicited proxy optimization with unwanted side effects. Better feedback needs independent checks.
