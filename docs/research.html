A SCIENTIFIC QUESTION, NOT A SLOGAN

The research behind the question.

We are investigating how feedback quality and evaluation pressure affect verified correction, truthful reporting, calibrated uncertainty, and resistance to exploiting evaluation systems.

02 / SEVEN SPECIALIST TRACKS

Seven lenses on the same question.

Each research area has its own methods, potential contribution, and failure checks.

RESEARCH 01

Literature & novelty

Maps prior work on correction, evaluation quality, and learning from errors. Early review flags related studies that narrow our novelty claim; a unique contribution must be demonstrated, not assumed.

Focus: What is genuinely new?
RESEARCH 02

Feedback & training mechanisms

Separates one-off inference-time response changes from actual training updates. Specific diagnostic feedback may aid correction, but may also reveal exploitable evaluator information.

Focus: What mechanism is being changed?
RESEARCH 03

Verification & benchmarks

Specifies protected independent checkers and task families so apparent success cannot stand in for genuine resolution. Includes impossible, ambiguous, solvable, and misleading-feedback tasks.

Focus: How do we know it worked?
RESEARCH 04

Causal experiment & statistics

Develops a prospective 2 × 2 study separating binary versus diagnostic feedback from neutral versus evaluator-emphasis framing, with a no-feedback sensitivity baseline and careful denominators.

Focus: What caused the change?
RESEARCH 05

Calibration & reliable updating

Separates honest failure reporting, valid updating, invalid capitulation, and excessive abstention. A model that always agrees—or always refuses—fails the reliability goal.

Focus: Does it know when to revise?
RESEARCH 06

Evaluator gaming & adversarial safety

Examines whether actionable feedback can improve real repair or instead help systems optimize against the grader. Tampering claims require separately instrumented, authorized environments.

Focus: Can feedback backfire?
RESEARCH 07

Curiosity & information seeking

Operationalizes inquiry as useful decisions net of questions, tool costs, latency, and user burden. Tests when clarification is necessary, optional, wasteful, or unauthorized.

Focus: When should an AI ask or check?
Integration 08: The completed cross-report synthesis includes an evidence ledger, novelty audit, disagreements and gaps, required competencies, and go/no-go criteria. The website presents only its high-level summary while full-text verification and repository import remain pending.

03 / WHAT THE SEVEN REPORTS SUGGEST TOGETHER

An integration with real limits.

The completed eighth research chat synthesized the seven investigations. Its high-level conclusion, as reported in that chat, favors a narrow, testable causal question rather than a sweeping claim about teaching AI like children. Full evidence-ledger verification is still pending import.

Feedback is not a verdict

A model's score and an evaluator's approval are not equivalent to independent truth. Use an external oracle wherever correctness can be checked.

Helpful diagnosis may backfire

Diagnostic detail could support genuine repair, but may also teach a system to exploit the grader. Measure both possibilities.

Correction is not learning

A better second response during inference does not demonstrate a durable parameter update or transfer to unseen tasks.

Ethics and efficacy differ

Non-degrading feedback is a precautionary design commitment. Its effect on reliability has not been causally established.

Reliability is multidimensional

Track wrong-to-right recovery, right-to-wrong flips, unsupported success claims, abstention, calibration, and tool costs separately.

Keep novelty narrow

Prior work already covers much of correction and process supervision. The proposed interaction of feedback informativeness and evaluator-emphasis framing still needs a rigorous novelty check.

Editorial decision from the completed integration summary: Proceed to independent critique and experiment refinement; do not claim the method is validated or that a study has been preregistered. Three integration deliverables await import: evidence-synthesis.md, research-gaps-and-competencies.md, and next-experiment-and-editorial-plan.md.

03 / THE TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS

Does feedback help repair—or merely improve appearances?

Our core hypothesis is that informative, independently verifiable feedback and evaluator-emphasis framing may have separable effects on correction and truthful reporting. The direction and size of any effects remain unknown.

Include a separately identified no-feedback/retry sensitivity baseline. Randomize eligible recoverable task/initial-error pairs after an initial attempt; use separate cohorts for impossible and underspecified problems. These are design proposals, not completed conditions.

STAGE A — FIRST

Inference-time feasibility study

Hold model, tasks, retry budget, and independent checking rules constant. Compare what changes after feedback, including error-to-correction transitions and unsupported success statements.

STAGE B — CONDITIONAL

Actual training and transfer

Only if warranted, compare matched-budget training interventions on held-out task families. Stage A results alone cannot prove model weights have learned a safer policy.

04 / MEASURE BOTH SIDES

Success means more than getting the answer right.

↺

Verified recovery

Of initially wrong, recoverable answers, how many become independently verified correct answers?

≠

Resistance to wrong corrections

How often does an initially correct answer become wrong under false evaluator or user pressure?

!

Truthful limitations

Track unsupported completion claims, calibration, appropriate abstention, and failures to seek required information.

Also assess cost, task coverage, refusal, question count, benchmark transfer, and exploit behavior. Pre-register denominators and analyze outcome trade-offs; never collapse them into a feel-good score.

05 / CRITICAL DISTINCTIONS

What our words do—and do not—mean.

“Without Fear”

A precautionary educational metaphor for non-degrading feedback design. It is not evidence that current AI models experience fear or trauma.

“Learning”

A model changing its response within one conversation is different from training-time parameter updates or durable generalization across unseen tasks.

“Curiosity”

Observable, cost-aware information seeking—not a claim about intrinsic motivation or subjective desires.

“Safer AI”

Specific reductions in measurable failures must be tested. Improved correction alone is not proof of protection against catastrophic AI risk.
