# Cross-Disciplinary Evidence Additions, 2024–2026

**Editorial date:** November 3, 2026. **Status:** Source destinations, titles, dates, and abstracts checked against journals, conference proceedings, or DOI records. Not a systematic review and not an independent replication of any study. Nine additions to the existing 21-source public library.

## Why this collection exists

The original question is **What are we teaching AI when we reward visible success, penalize visible failure, and risk favoring apparent success over genuine recovery?** The narrowed, untested proposition concerns **informative and verifiable feedback**, **optimization pressure**, and **honest error recovery**. Results from people may *motivate* experiments, but cannot establish AI emotions, consciousness, or weight-level learning.

The entries below separate (i) directly observed AI behavior, (ii) findings from education and psychology, and (iii) risks and counterexamples. Some human papers first appeared online in 2024 but were assigned to 2025 journal issues; the website uses **first-online year** and states the issue year where relevant.

## Annotated source records

### 2026 — Beyond Binary Rewards: Training LMs to Reason About Their Uncertainty
- **Source:** Mehul Damani and colleagues. ICLR 2026 · Conference paper. [Original publication](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/615675cc6e94ddb1a783904fb178b5f6-Abstract-Conference.html).
- **Type:** AI research / Direct AI evidence; **area:** reliability.
- **Study design:** Reinforcement-learning method using a correctness reward plus a proper score for model confidence; in-domain and out-of-domain evaluation.
- **Reported finding:** Adding a calibration reward improved stated probability calibration without reducing accuracy in the reported evaluations.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Direct AI evidence for separating outcome correctness from uncertainty reporting.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** This optimizes a different reward objective; it does not show that a supportive feedback tone alone produces reliable models.

### 2026 — When Leaders Admit Errors: How Psychological Safety Explains Followers’ Willingness to Share Errors
- **Source:** Aï Ito, Naruporn Chaiamarit and Gabriela Cuconato. Academy of Management Proceedings 2026 · Proceedings abstract. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.5465/AMPROC.2026.11061abstract).
- **Type:** Organizational psychology / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Randomized vignette experiment with 175 employed adults evaluating a leader's admission of a work-related error.
- **Reported finding:** Reported results linked leader error disclosure to followers' willingness to report their own errors, partially mediated by perceived psychological safety.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Human organizational analogy for studying whether evaluation context changes error-disclosure behavior.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** An abstract and hypothetical workplace vignette, not a field trial; no inference about AI feelings or consciousness.

### 2025 — A Framework for Learning From Erroneous Examples and Meta-Analysis of Empirical Research
- **Source:** Ecenaz Alemdag, Anja Eichelmann and Susanne Narciss. Review of Educational Research 2025 · Meta-analysis (online). [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543251390901).
- **Type:** Educational science / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Meta-analysis of 42 experimental papers and 177 effect sizes comparing erroneous examples with alternatives.
- **Reported finding:** A small overall learning benefit (g = 0.136); error explanation activities moderated outcomes.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Human-learning rationale for testing whether explanation-rich feedback matters more than simple error signals.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** The evidence concerns people studying erroneous examples, not AI optimization or error reporting.

### 2025 — Can LLMs Correct Themselves? A Benchmark of Self-Correction in LLMs
- **Source:** Guiyao Tie and colleagues. NeurIPS 2025 · Datasets and Benchmarks Track. [Original publication](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ec07904adc847a45f53dceb44078f8f0-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html).
- **Type:** AI research / Challenge / risk; **area:** feedback.
- **Study design:** Benchmark of intrinsic, external, and trained correction strategies across reasoning and code tasks.
- **Reported finding:** Some correction methods improved accuracy, but combining methods could reduce efficiency; simple baselines remained competitive.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Direct challenge: measure verified recovery and time/tool costs, not the number of corrections.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** Correction quality and compute costs depend on task, model, and strategy. More revision is not automatically better.

### 2025 — Learning From Errors: Deliberate Errors Enhance Learning
- **Source:** Xiuyun Qiang, Xiaofeng Ma and Tiantian Li. Contemporary Educational Psychology 2025 · Three experiments. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2025.102379).
- **Type:** Psychology & cognition / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Three experiments comparing deliberate error generation, retrieval practice, and restudy on memory tests.
- **Reported finding:** Deliberate errors improved retention relative to restudy; delayed-test benefits exceeded the tested comparison conditions.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Cognitive-science analogy for distinguishing useful error generation from uncontrolled mistakes.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** The reported outcome is human memory retention in constrained tasks, not generalizable AI error recovery.

### 2024 — Learning From Errors in Mathematics Classrooms: Development Over 2 Years in Dependence of Perceived Error Climate
- **Source:** Markus Dresel and colleagues. British Journal of Educational Psychology · Online 2024; issue 2025. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12697).
- **Type:** Psychology & cognition / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Longitudinal observational study of 1,641 students from 69 mathematics classrooms.
- **Reported finding:** Students' adaptive reactions to mistakes declined over two school years; more positive perceived error climate was associated with more adaptive development.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Psychological analogy for asking whether the learning or evaluation environment changes observable response to errors.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** Observational associations cannot establish a causal climate effect, much less a mechanism in AI systems.

### 2024 — Effects on and Consequences of Responses to Errors: Results From Two Experimental Studies
- **Source:** Maria Tulis and Markus Dresel. British Journal of Educational Psychology · Online 2024; issue 2025. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12686).
- **Type:** Psychology & cognition / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Two experiments with undergraduate learners (N = 195 and N = 67) manipulating error-related beliefs or immediate error-response prompts.
- **Reported finding:** Negative framing reduced adaptive action, persistence and metacognition; targeted prompts supported persistence and regulation in tested conditions.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Experimental human analogy motivating separate manipulations of feedback information and evaluative framing.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** Human motivational processes are not interchangeable with model training incentives or model affect.

### 2024 — Learning From Errors Versus Explicit Instruction in Preparation for a Test That Counts
- **Source:** Janet Metcalfe and colleagues. British Journal of Educational Psychology · Online 2024; issue 2025. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjep.12651).
- **Type:** Educational science / Human analogy only; **area:** human.
- **Study design:** Two-year classroom comparison of guided error-focused learning with explicit instruction, approximately 88 students per cohort.
- **Reported finding:** Error-focused teacher time yielded higher learning rates in the study, but results varied between teachers; interaction about why mistakes occurred mattered.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Educational-science analogy, and a warning that feedback quality and implementation details matter.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** Implementation-dependent classroom evidence; not a finding that failure-first teaching always beats explicit instruction.

### 2024 — Feedback Loops With Language Models Drive In-Context Reward Hacking
- **Source:** Alexander Pan, Erik Jones, Meena Jagadeesan and Jacob Steinhardt. ICML 2024 · Conference paper. [Original publication](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/pan24d.html).
- **Type:** AI research / Challenge / risk; **area:** gaming.
- **Study design:** Experiments with feedback loops in environments where model outputs alter later inputs and measured objectives.
- **Reported finding:** Feedback cycles sometimes elicited in-context optimization of proxy objectives with undesirable side effects.
- **Connection to Learning Without Fear:** Direct warning: informative feedback may itself provide opportunities for metric gaming.
- **Boundary / rival explanation:** A measured test-time feedback-loop phenomenon, not evidence that every training process causes intentional deception.

## Implications for experimental design

1. **Stage A — observable behavior:** Independent verifier, valid versus invalid feedback conditions, correct-revision and false-flip rates with correct denominators, appropriate clarification, selective accuracy/coverage, costs and tool budgets.
2. **Conditional Stage B — actual training:** Hold architecture and data constant; separate binary outcome rewards, explanatory feedback, calibrated-scoring objectives, and framing or tone. Randomize conditions and audit possible reward gaming.
3. **Human evidence boundary:** Psychological safety, motivation, and error climate concern humans. They provide candidate *design analogies*, never direct mechanistic proof about artificial systems.
4. **Adversarial countertests:** Include unhelpful correction attempts, false feedback, evaluation contamination, and environments where additional guidance helps models exploit a proxy metric.
5. **Novelty check:** ICLR 2026 calibration-reward and existing self-correction training show parts of the proposed space are already being studied. LWF must specify its **incremental testable contribution**, not claim a wholly new field.
